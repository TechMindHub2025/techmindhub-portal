[Vodafone opened a European AI-RAN testing hub in Málaga](https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/technology/malaga-lab-ai-ran) as [VodafoneThree announced planned UK laboratory and field trials](https://www.vodafonethree.com/nokia-trial-ai-ran-nvidia-platform/) on November 18. The two efforts ask related engineering questions: can AI improve radio access performance, and can radio functions and AI workloads share the same compute platform effectively?

The Málaga lab brings together Nokia, NVIDIA, Intel and other partners. Vodafone says its equipment includes NVIDIA Aerial RAN Computer servers and Intel Xeon 6 systems. It intends to compare accelerated systems, general-purpose processors and telecom ASIC approaches using repeatable benchmarks rather than assuming one architecture wins every workload.

## Three distinct experiments

**AI-for-RAN** applies algorithms to radio tasks such as channel estimation, link adaptation, beamforming and energy management. **AI-and-RAN** examines shared compute capacity for radio and AI workloads, raising questions about scheduling, isolation, latency, power and cost. **AI-on-RAN** asks whether distributed network sites can host applications closer to users. These categories are research programs, not confirmed deployment outcomes.

Vodafone plans a massive-MIMO laboratory call in December 2026, followed by field trials. VodafoneThree's separate UK program with Nokia and NVIDIA will also move from controlled testing to selected live sites. Claims about higher spectral efficiency or improved user experience therefore remain hypotheses to test against current networks. The companies have not published independent head-to-head performance results for these announced programs.

A useful evaluation should record not only throughput and radio quality but also accelerator utilization, power per delivered bit, contention between workloads and the failure behavior when a shared node becomes unavailable. The lab's importance lies in making these trade-offs measurable before an AI-native radio architecture is deployed widely.
