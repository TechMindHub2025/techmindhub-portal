The accelerators in an AI data centre receive attention because they do the visible computation. Their power supplies have a different job: turn an incoming electrical supply into usable rails while load, temperature and fault conditions change. The control electronics in that conversion path do not run the AI model, yet they can influence conversion losses, response to transients and the heat the facility must remove. STMicroelectronics' [STM32G5 announcement](https://newsroom.st.com/media-center/press-item.html/stmicroelectronics-reveals-advanced-microcontrollers-for-real-time-power-control-in-ai-datacenters-and-robotics-p4797.html), dated November 4, 2026, is best understood in that less visible layer.

ST describes a mixed-signal microcontroller family built around a 280 MHz Arm Cortex-M33. It claims twice the compute performance of STM32G4 and highlights an enhanced high-resolution timer, a position encoder, a programmable logic array and hardware security. Single-core products form the announced series; dual-core variants are planned for 2027. The company's headline connects the same control platform to data-centre power supplies and robotic motors. Those are applications, not evidence that a particular installation has already reduced energy consumption.

## What the controller actually controls

A digitally controlled converter measures relevant electrical quantities, computes a response and schedules switching events in its power stage. The controller's timer determines when those events occur; analog sensing and the associated signal path determine what the controller knows about the output. The switching devices, magnetic components, layout, cooling and protection circuitry determine much of what happens next. A faster CPU is useful only if the control loop can make effective decisions at the necessary rate and its measurements and actuation are sufficiently accurate.

ST specifically argues that the high-resolution timer can support algorithms that adjust a supply as server demand varies. It also connects finer switching control to power supplies using silicon-carbide and gallium-nitride devices, which can operate at higher switching frequencies. That is a plausible design opportunity, but increasing frequency is not a free efficiency gain. It can reduce some passive-component size while increasing switching-related losses, electromagnetic-interference demands and sensitivity to timing and layout. An engineer would need to measure the complete converter at the intended load profile, not infer its efficiency from the timer specification.

There is a related distinction between control resolution and useful system resolution. A precise timer cannot repair a noisy current measurement, late ADC sample or unstable feedback design. Nor can control software remove heat generated by the power stage. For a rack supply, the appropriate comparison holds the same input conditions, output power, load transitions, thermal environment and reliability requirements constant. Report efficiency over the relevant load range, response to a step in demand, protective behaviour and total losses. A single peak-efficiency number can hide the part of the curve where the installation spends most of its time.

## The motor-control connection

The same family is aimed at electric motors in robots and industrial drives. Here the controller measures position or electrical state, calculates a control response and drives one or more axes. ST points to the position encoder and upgraded peripherals as part of its case; a quoted partner discusses dexterous hands with many motors. These are not interchangeable with the data-centre use case. Motor control introduces coupled mechanical loads, position feedback, torque limits and fault recovery. An MCU may support several loops, but the number of motors that can be controlled well depends on the loop rates, sensing architecture, communications and safety case of a particular design.

The useful commonality is a real-time scheduling problem. A controller must complete sampling, computation and actuation within known windows. Average CPU utilization alone will not reveal an occasional missed update at the moment a converter changes load or a robot contacts an object. A prototype should record worst-case loop timing under communications, diagnostics and fault handling, then examine the resulting physical output. The stronger the consequence of a missed or erroneous update, the more important independent protection becomes.

ST also presents security provisions, including Arm TrustZone, its immutable root-of-trust feature for secure boot and firmware update, secure storage and cryptographic support. These matter because a networked power or motor controller can have a long service life. The announcement describes capabilities; a deployed device still needs a provisioning process, protected update keys, recovery procedures and evidence that the chosen firmware configuration uses those features as intended. A secure-boot building block is not the same as an evaluated facility security architecture.

## How to evaluate the claim

The reported twofold compute improvement is a manufacturer comparison with STM32G4. It is not a measurement of twofold converter efficiency or a claim that a robotic hand moves twice as precisely. For a candidate design, start with the current controller's actual bottleneck: control-loop computation, event timing, analog acquisition, memory, interfaces or protection response. If it is not compute-bound, the headline comparison may be less relevant than the peripheral and integration changes.

An evaluation board and a representative power stage or drive can then provide evidence. Measure end-to-end latency from sensed event to switching response, jitter during worst-case concurrent work, conversion losses across operating points and recovery after defined faults. Record the hardware revision, control parameters and firmware alongside results. For a data centre, include maintenance and serviceability: software updates and replacement units must preserve the electrical behaviour already qualified. For a robot, include multi-axis coordination and the failure mode when a sensor becomes unavailable.

The engineering opportunity is real enough to investigate: precise digital control can adapt power conversion and motors to changing conditions. The released information establishes a new control platform and the vendor's intended applications. Whether it improves a facility's energy balance or a robot's reliability remains a measured property of the complete design.
