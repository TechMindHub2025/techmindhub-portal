An autonomous construction machine is not simply an ordinary vehicle with a missing driver. The cab, local sensing, dispatch link, safety perimeter and human escalation path all affect how it can work. In its [November 24 bauma CHINA announcement](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/sany-advances-toward-an-ai-native-robotics-future-at-bauma-china-2026-302949365.html), SANY grouped several developments under an ambition to build more robotic equipment. The examples differ materially in maturity.

## Machine: autonomy changes the platform

The company introduced the **SKT165Ei**, a cabless, battery-electric mining haul truck in the 100-tonne class. SANY says it can load, haul and dump under cloud dispatch, including with weak network coverage. It also displayed an autonomous wheel loader and described **E-MOVE** as an early electric excavator robot prototype. A trade-show demonstration and a prototype are evidence of a design direction; neither establishes an independently measured safety case across unstructured job sites.

Cab removal can change the payload and packaging budget, but it also moves responsibility for obstacle detection, stopping, site access and exception handling to sensors, onboard control and remote operations. For a useful evaluation, a fleet operator would need the operating design domain: terrain, dust, visibility, geofencing, network loss behavior and procedures for people entering a work zone. The release's claims about charging time and payload ratio use company-defined comparisons and are not universal machine characteristics.

## Fleet: coordination is another problem

SANY says its driverless paving fleets have worked on more than **60 projects** since 2019, with one human operator supervising up to **15 machines**. It presents a new cabless electric roller, **SSR360C-10E**, as part of that coordinated approach. These figures are supplier-reported and do not specify the full conditions, intervention rates or independently audited comparison. A paving convoy has shared dependencies: if a paver slows, rollers must adapt; if the task or perimeter changes, a supervisor needs a clear way to pause the group.

At the operations layer, SANY also announced its **SanVIST** connectivity platform and an assistant for project operations, work guidance, repair diagnosis and task coordination. A management interface does not imply autonomous safe action by every connected vehicle. The article illustration depicts a conceptual progression, not a faithful layout of a SANY work site or product interface.

The engineering question is where machine-level autonomy ends and fleet-level supervision begins. Manufacturers can announce capable hardware and report real-world mileage; deployment decisions still require site-specific hazard analysis, documented human authority, failure-mode tests and measured productivity against a comparable conventional operation.
