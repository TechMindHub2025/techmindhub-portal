Robotic construction is often framed as a demand for better positioning. MIT researcher Miana Smith's [work on self-aligning structural voxels](https://news.mit.edu/2026/building-blocks-for-greener-skyline-miana-smith-1118) turns the question around: what if the building block itself helped correct placement error? The November 18 MIT feature describes modular elements whose geometry guides assembly even when a robot delivers them less precisely than a conventional industrial assembly process might require.

## Tolerance as a design resource

An assembly robot has finite positioning accuracy, especially as the work envelope grows. A shaped interface can convert a modest lateral placement error into guided contact, bringing mating elements toward the intended alignment. That shifts part of the burden from sensing and control into the physical design of the component. It does not eliminate tolerance: joint geometry, surface finish, friction, part variation and accumulated error determine whether the guidance works repeatedly.

The MIT account contrasts robotic placement around **10 millimeters** with industrial positioning on the order of **100 micrometers**. These are contextual scales in a research narrative, not a claim that every robot or every proposed block meets those exact specifications. The relevant comparison is the total assembly system: gripper, perception, block manufacturing, interfaces and inspection after placement.

## What construction would still need to prove

A mechanically elegant voxel is only one part of a building. Structural capacity under static and cyclic loads, connection durability, fire and weather performance, manufacturability, disassembly and construction codes all matter. A full-scale economic comparison must also include transport and assembly time, not just a potentially simpler robot.

MIT's article is a **research profile**, not an independent field trial of a multi-story building. The lesson is useful beyond construction: embodied intelligence can reside in mating geometry as well as software. Whether that trade makes a real structure cheaper or greener awaits reproducible structural and lifecycle evidence.
