Much of a nuclear project's uncertainty appears before construction: finding a suitable site, reconciling infrastructure and environmental constraints, selecting a reactor technology, and preparing for licensing. On November 17, [Elementl Power announced a collaboration with Google Cloud](https://elementlpower.io/newsroom/111726/google-cloud) to build **Lattice**, a suite of tools for this preconstruction work.

The first tool, **Atlas**, assesses hundreds of site characteristics against geographic, environmental and infrastructure requirements. Elementl says it can examine multiple candidate locations and reactor designs across large areas. The company describes compressing preliminary desktop research from months into days or weeks. That is its process claim, not an independently benchmarked reduction in total project duration.

## What a site shortlist must preserve

A credible screening system needs traceable data provenance, current grid and water assumptions, explainable exclusion rules and a record of uncertainty. A favorable map overlay is a starting point for investigation, not evidence that a site will obtain permits, a grid connection, local acceptance or a successful safety case. Human engineers and regulators must still validate the underlying facts and decisions.

Elementl says Lattice uses Gemini for business and BigQuery and is intended to capture lessons from actual project development. Additional capabilities for a controlled design and licensing record remain under development. The announced collaboration thus spans an available first tool and future plans; neither should be confused with a completed nuclear plant or a licensed design.

The engineering value would be in reducing repeated manual assembly of fragmented inputs while preserving an auditable path from data to a shortlist. Independent comparisons of accuracy, false exclusions, time saved and downstream project outcomes have not been published in the announcement.
