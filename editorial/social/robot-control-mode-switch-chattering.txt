Smoother Robot Control Starts at the Transition Between Modes

A Scientific Reports study examines chattering when a hybrid robot controller switches between admittance and adaptive impedance control. The authors propose an integral resonant filter in the switching loop.

In a comparative simulation of a two-joint planar rehabilitation robot, they report more than 76% average reduction in their Chattering Index for both joints against a hysteresis-based method, while maintaining trajectory tracking accuracy. This was a simulation result. It does not establish hardware performance or clinical benefit.

Our engineering assessment: measure the transition itself. Track commanded torque, contact force and tracking error around switching events. A filter should reduce rapid command changes without making the response too slow for the task.

A hardware test needs matched trajectories, contact conditions, sampling rates and actuator limits, with filter settings documented. The reported percentage is a useful starting point for that experiment, not a general guarantee.
