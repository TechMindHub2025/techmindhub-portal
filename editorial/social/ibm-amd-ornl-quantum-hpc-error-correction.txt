Quantum Computing Meets HPC: IBM, AMD and Oak Ridge Build a Co-Design Platform

IBM Quantum Nighthawk r2 processors now share a research environment with Oak Ridge's Umbra HPC system, which uses AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and fifth-generation EPYC CPUs. The systems are co-located at IBM's Poughkeepsie site to study quantum error correction and other hybrid workflows.

The central engineering problem is timing. Measurements from a quantum processor need classical interpretation; a decoder must keep up if later operations depend on its results. A fast GPU kernel alone is insufficient if data acquisition, transfer and control handoffs dominate the feedback loop.

This is a deployed research platform, not a completed fault-tolerant quantum computer. IBM's Starling target for 2029 remains a plan, and the launch article does not include an independent benchmark of the new platform's decoding latency.
