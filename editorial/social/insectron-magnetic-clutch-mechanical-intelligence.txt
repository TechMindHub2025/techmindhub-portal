When Robot Mechanics Become Intelligence: INSECTRON's Magnetic Clutches

The INSECTRON prototype is a 33.9 g, six-legged robot driven by two motors. Magnetic couplings connect its legs to the drivetrain: they transmit motion in ordinary walking, yield under overload and can re-engage after a disturbance. Hall-effect sensing still coordinates the two drive modules. The interesting result comes from the partnership between mechanical response and feedback control.

In three controlled vertical perturbation conditions, the researchers reported recovery probabilities of 91%, 95% and 94%. Those results do not predict recovery after every real-world collision. Forward speed also dropped from 2.7 cm/s on the flat reference surface to 0.8 cm/s on a 5-degree incline.

Our analysis examines the clutch threshold, the limited scope of the recovery experiment, and why a miniature robot may benefit from putting part of its response in the body.
