High-density AI infrastructure is forcing power delivery and thermal design into the same engineering conversation.

Delta's SC26 announcement brings this into focus: an 800 VDC-powered 2.5 MW liquid-to-liquid CDU, emerging 800 VDC and ±400 VDC distribution architectures, and liquid cooling for power components alongside compute.

Higher voltage can reduce current and conductor losses. It does not, on its own, prove better end-to-end facility efficiency. Pump energy, conversion stages, protection and the required coolant temperatures remain part of the same equation.

There is also an availability question: the CDU announcement specifies three hot-swappable pumps in a 3+0 arrangement. Hot swapping is not equivalent to proven N+1 resilience at full load.

The analysis examines the hydraulic and electrical boundaries that would need independent validation before this becomes a deployment standard.
