Stanford researchers put a roughly half-millimeter, light-powered wireless sensor inside a reaction vessel. It can report temperature about every half-second, offering a time-resolved view across many small experiments.

Temperature can signal reaction dynamics, but it does not identify chemical composition directly. Optical access, packaging, calibration and chemical compatibility remain practical tests. Where would continuous in-vessel telemetry change your experimental workflow?
