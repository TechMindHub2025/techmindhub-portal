Liquid cooling in the tropics is a reliability problem as much as a heat-removal problem.

Singapore introduced SS 726:2026 in August to address direct liquid cooling in tropical data centers. Official public material discusses cooling-loop design, coolant quality, material selection, commissioning, monitoring, corrosion and microbial risks.

One important boundary: the published scope explicitly excludes rear-door heat exchangers and two-phase or hybrid single-/two-phase systems. The standard is not a universal approval for every water-cooled rack.

The full standard is a paid 70-page document. The analysis distinguishes what the public scope and government factsheet establish from practical engineering considerations such as condensation, fluid monitoring and safe maintenance.

This is a standards analysis, not a new November launch or a claim of mandatory certification.
