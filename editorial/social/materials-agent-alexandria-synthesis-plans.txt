A promising computed material is only the beginning of a laboratory question. A scientific agent described in a new preprint connects the Alexandria database, structure models and a reaction graph to screen candidates and propose synthesis routes.

The environment includes about 15 million DFT calculations and a graph of 17.4 billion possible reactions. These counts describe computational resources, not materials discovered or experiments completed. The agent can reject an unsuitable route and write a testable plan with proposed precursors and conditions.

No successful synthesis follows automatically from that plan. We examine how to keep the chain from calculation to proposed experiment inspectable, and why laboratory validation remains essential.
