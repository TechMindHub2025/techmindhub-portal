AI-Designed Robot Routing: From Generated Code to Operational Evidence

What evidence would your organisation require before replacing a proven fleet-coordination algorithm?

A preprint submitted on October 19 and revised on October 20, 2026 explores “policy search in code space.” Researchers from KNAPP and several universities describe humans directing objectives and constraints while coding agents modify coordination programs and investigate simulation results.

The resulting planner runs as compiled software. Language-model inference is confined to development.

On one simulated warehouse layout, the authors report 58.9% higher throughput than a calibrated proprietary baseline, measured across eight paired two-hour runs. This is evidence for that setting, not a general warehouse productivity forecast. Comparisons on other layouts used an uncalibrated baseline and require a different interpretation.

The authors also present a conditional progress guarantee and acknowledge that generated implementations can become difficult to audit and maintain. The work is a preprint; the reported gains come from simulation.

Our assessment: separating algorithm discovery from operational execution creates a useful engineering boundary. Teams can preserve candidate versions and evaluate a proposed planner as a software change.

The difficult part is representing the real operation well enough. Alongside mean throughput, a trial should examine disruption, congestion recovery, equipment unavailability and changing workloads. Formal assumptions need to be mapped onto the installation.

A practical next step would be historical workload replay, controlled testing and a defined rollback process. Maintenance effort belongs in the evaluation alongside performance.
