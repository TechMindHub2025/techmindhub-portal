A Nature Materials study reports free-standing atomically thin metal oxides made by removing halogen atoms from a layered precursor with a focused electron beam. In CrOCl, chlorine removal produces CrO; the team reports single-crystal areas above 10,000 nm² and micrometer-scale patterning.

The microscope becomes a local fabrication tool. The difficult next step is connecting that structural precision to measured properties, usable devices and repeatable throughput. A calculated prediction of antiferromagnetic ordering below 230 K is not a direct magnetic measurement of the fabricated sheet.

The result opens an experimental route, while yield, stability, contacts and manufacturing cost remain separate questions.

Which measurement would most clearly move this from a material specimen toward a working device?
