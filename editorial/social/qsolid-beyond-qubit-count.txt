QSolid: Scaling Quantum Computers Beyond the Qubit Count

Fraunhofer IPMS reports two distinct QSolid milestones: a tested 10-qubit system accessible to users, and a 30-qubit system still under testing. The announcement also describes work on cryogenic CMOS control circuits and interposers connecting quantum and control chips.

Our assessment: scaling a quantum computer is a systems-engineering problem. More qubits increase the demands on signal delivery, calibration, physical connections and the thermal budget. A component result, an assembled demonstrator and an application run are different forms of evidence.

For advanced packaging, useful questions include signal behaviour at the intended temperature, heat loading and repeatability after assembly and cooling cycles. For a machine, ask what was tested together, under which operating conditions and with what errors. These are evaluation criteria, not results claimed by the consortium.

Industrial semiconductor methods may help make assembly repeatable. The announcement does not yet establish manufacturing yield, a computational advantage over classical systems or fault tolerance.

When assessing a quantum milestone, what would you want to know beyond the number of qubits?
