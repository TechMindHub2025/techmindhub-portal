VigilSOC 1.0.0, released October 29, brings specialized AI agents into security operations. Its announcement describes investigation and response workflows connected through MCP, with configurable approval mechanisms.

The important question is where an agent's authority ends. Reading telemetry, proposing a response and disabling an account carry different consequences. A useful trial should trace every tool call to the evidence and permission that justified it, then test stale alerts, untrusted text and failed integrations.

General Availability is the project's release designation, not an independent reliability benchmark. OpenCTI integration and federated detection are on its roadmap.

Which security action would you allow only after a human has examined the evidence?
