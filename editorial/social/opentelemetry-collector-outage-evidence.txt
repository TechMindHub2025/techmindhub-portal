OpenTelemetry Collector: Can Your Monitoring Data Survive an Outage?

Collector binary distributions v0.163.2 updated their Go version and CA bundle. The practical question this established project raises is broader: what happens to monitoring evidence when an exporter cannot reach its backend?

The Collector can queue and retry telemetry. Those mechanisms have limits. A queue can fill, a retry window can expire, and an in-memory queue loses its contents if the Collector process crashes. Persistent storage can help queued data survive a restart, while introducing a disk dependency.

Our assessment: test one traces pipeline in a safe environment. Send a counted set of spans without sampling. Interrupt the backend, restore it and reconcile the identifiers that arrive. Repeat with a Collector restart while data is queued. Record queue occupancy, missing or duplicate observations, delivery delay and resource use. Choose the acceptable result before running the experiment.

The v0.163.2 release did not introduce these resilience mechanisms; the official documentation describes them. The point of the test is to learn how your selected distribution and configuration behave under a measured interruption.

How many missing spans—and how much delay—would your team accept during a backend outage?
