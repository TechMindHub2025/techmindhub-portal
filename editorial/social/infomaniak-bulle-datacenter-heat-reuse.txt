Can a 2.5 MW data center deliver 28 GWh of district heating in a year?

Infomaniak and Gruyère Energie have proposed a 170-rack data center in Bulle, Switzerland, with closed-loop liquid cooling and heat pumps connecting server waste heat to the local heating network. Commissioning is targeted for 2028, subject to approvals.

The 28 GWh figure is a forecast, not measured output. For comparison, a 2.5 MW IT load running at full power every hour of a year would consume 21.9 GWh. The larger heating forecast is not impossible: heat-pump electricity contributes to delivered heat. But the published announcement does not yet give the full annual energy balance, seasonal COP or demand profile.

Emissions accounting matters too. The projected 7,500 tonnes of CO2 avoided is measured against a heating-oil scenario, not necessarily against the existing, predominantly renewable district network.

Our engineering analysis asks which meters and operating data would make this project genuinely verifiable.
