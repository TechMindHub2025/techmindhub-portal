Google Threat Intelligence Group reports a joint disruption of XCPE, a backdoor linked to more than 1.3 million Android TV set-top boxes. Google assesses with high confidence that Videostrong intentionally deployed and operated it; the reported code loaded malicious functionality after first boot.

The trust problem starts before a customer installs anything. Signed firmware and a passed setup test have defined limits if a vendor-controlled component is itself malicious or activates later. Device inventories, independent reference measurements and post-boot network observation become important.

Disrupting command infrastructure can reduce abuse, but it does not prove that every compromised box has been cleaned.
